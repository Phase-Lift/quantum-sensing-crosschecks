# Sensor commutator checkpoint v0.2

**Status:** Conditional analytical derivation, symbolic verification, finite-matrix pulse simulation, and classical sampling of specified quantum probabilities. **Not a hardware experiment, a microscopic theory of quantum gravity, or a sub-Planck observation.**

## Executive result

The previous GHZ benchmark encoded a phase without deriving its physical origin. This extension specifies a candidate effective commutator and actual ideal pulse Hamiltonians. A 32-oscillator-pulse sequence maps their deformation into a three-qubit phase while canceling the ordinary quadratic loop contribution:

\[
\Phi_{\rm fleet}=128\beta a^3b\,q_0g_1g_2g_3+O(\beta^2).
\]

This is a conditional interaction-to-observable bridge. It does not derive the chosen commutator from an independently specified geometric theory or any ultraviolet completion.

An exact counterexample also exists: ordinary canonical mechanics with a cubic momentum dependence in the control Hamiltonian produces precisely the same modeled pulse channel. Thus the protocol measures an **effective nonlinear coefficient**, not a uniquely attributable quantum-gravity parameter. Increasing the number of shots does not resolve that degeneracy.

**Results:** the algebra and ideal pulse simulation pass; nuisance filtering passes within its stated class; exclusive microscopic attribution fails; practical Planck-scale sensitivity is not established. The best next experimental question concerns independently characterized pulse and oscillator Hamiltonians, not simply more sensors or repetitions.

## 1. Physical hypotheses and conventions

Use dimensionless mechanical quadratures, with a candidate deformation

\[
[X,P]=i(1+\beta P^2).
\]

This is a phenomenological generalized-uncertainty-principle (GUP) model, not an established property of nature. A first-order canonical representation is

\[
X=x,\qquad P=p+\frac{\beta}{3}p^3+O(\beta^2),\qquad [x,p]=i.
\]

Indeed,

\[
[x,p+\beta p^3/3]=i(1+\beta p^2)=i(1+\beta P^2)+O(\beta^2).
\]

The polynomial model \(P=p+\beta p^3/3\) is itself a perfectly ordinary canonical quantum-control model and can be simulated exactly. Its interpretation as the stated GUP is only a first-order approximation. A truncated polynomial is **not** by itself a full minimal-length theory valid at arbitrarily large momentum.

For three qubits define

\[
Q=q_0 I+g_1Z_1+g_2Z_2+g_3Z_3.
\]

Here \(q_0\) is a common, independently adjustable drive bias; \(g_i\) are conditional couplings; and \(Z_i\) have eigenvalues \(\pm1\). The oscillator couples to all three qubits. Spatial distribution is not required, and no advantage over an integrated instrument is claimed.

Assume access to signed, short pulses generated by

\[
H_X(t)=-\hbar u_X(t) QX,\qquad
H_P(t)=-\hbar u_P(t) QP.
\]

Their time integrals are dimensionless pulse areas. A pulse with area \(a\) gives \(e^{iaQX}\), and one with area \(b\) gives \(e^{ibQP}\). The model assumes direct calibrated momentum coupling. Synthesizing this through free oscillator evolution is a different physical implementation and requires rederiving the corrections; it cannot simply inherit the answer below.

Additional assumptions: negligible free evolution and dissipation during the modeled pulses; ideal qubit flips; commuting longitudinal couplings; calibrated phase reference; identical prescribed pulse strengths between control blocks; retained shot identities; and a known readout visibility. Hardware capable of all these assumptions has not been specified.

## 2. One commutator loop

Let

\[
L(a,b;Q)=e^{iaQx}e^{ibQP}e^{-iaQx}e^{-ibQP}.
\]

The rightmost pulse acts first. Since

\[
e^{iaQx}p e^{-iaQx}=p-aQ,
\]

and functions of \(p\) commute, the polynomial representation gives

\[
L(a,b;Q)=\exp\{ibQ[P(p-aQ)-P(p)]\}.
\]

For \(P=p+\beta p^3/3\), this is exactly

\[
L=\exp\left[-iabQ^2-i\beta abQ^2p^2
+i\beta a^2bQ^3p-\frac{i\beta}{3}a^3bQ^4\right].
\]

For \(\beta=0\), the oscillator disappears from the loop channel and only the usual quadratic conditional phase remains. This basic commutator-to-phase strategy is established prior art [1].

## 3. Signed loop pair removes the odd momentum term

Apply two loops,

\[
S(Q)=L(-a,-b;Q)L(a,b;Q).
\]

Changing both signs preserves the oriented area; it is not the same as reversing the loop's orientation. The \(Q^3p\) term cancels and the remaining exponents commute:

\[
S(Q)=\exp\left[-i2abQ^2(1+\beta p^2)
-i\frac{2\beta}{3}a^3bQ^4\right].
\]

This uses eight oscillator pulses. Under the polynomial model it is exact; under the full hypothesized GUP it is the retained first-order expression.

## 4. Four sign settings isolate a three-body phase

Use the following four toggling frames, implemented by flipping pairs of qubits:

| Frame | \(s_1\) | \(s_2\) | \(s_3\) | Product |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(I\) | +1 | +1 | +1 | +1 |
| \(X_2X_3\) | +1 | -1 | -1 | +1 |
| \(X_1X_3\) | -1 | +1 | -1 | +1 |
| \(X_1X_2\) | -1 | -1 | +1 | +1 |

In each frame perform one signed loop pair with

\[
Q_s=q_0I+\sum_i g_is_iZ_i.
\]

A closing physical sequence uses pair flips \(X_2X_3,X_1X_2,X_2X_3,X_1X_2\) between frames and at the end. That is eight individual qubit X gates in addition to the 32 oscillator pulses. Preparation and readout operations have additional costs.

The signs obey

\[
\sum_s s_i=0,\quad \sum_s s_is_j=0\ (i\ne j),\quad
\sum_s s_1s_2s_3=4.
\]

Therefore,

\[
\sum_s Q_s^2=4\left(q_0^2+\sum_i g_i^2\right)I,
\]

\[
\sum_s Q_s^4=4C_0I+96q_0g_1g_2g_3Z_1Z_2Z_3,
\]

where

\[
C_0=q_0^4+6q_0^2\sum_i g_i^2+\sum_i g_i^4
+6\sum_{i<j}g_i^2g_j^2.
\]

All the exponents are diagonal in qubit Z and oscillator momentum, so they commute. The complete channel factors:

\[
U_{\rm full}=U_{\rm osc}\otimes
\exp[-i64\beta a^3bq_0g_1g_2g_3Z_1Z_2Z_3].
\]

The oscillator factor is identical for every qubit branch. It need not return the oscillator to its original state, but it does not carry branch information in this retained model. The spin phase is therefore independent of the initial oscillator momentum distribution at first order.

This is an instance of group symmetrization, a known quantum-control method [3], rather than a new general decoupling principle. Novelty of this particular composition has not been established by an exhaustive prior-art search.

### Nuisances removed, and nuisances not removed

An equal-duration, time-independent diagonal nuisance

\[
H_{\rm nuisance}=h_0 I+\sum_i h_i Z_i+\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}Z_iZ_j
\]

has only a common phase left after the same sign cycle, assuming it commutes with the pulse Hamiltonians. The cancellation does not require the coefficients to be small.

This does **not** remove arbitrary noise. Noncommuting disturbances, time-varying coefficients, miscalibrated pulse areas, finite-duration qubit flips, correlated readout errors, and real three-body terms need separate treatment. Readout visibility is imposed rather than derived from a complete dissipative hardware model.

## 5. Connecting to the earlier GHZ fleet readout

Prepare

\[
|\mathrm{GHZ}\rangle=(|000\rangle+|111\rangle)/\sqrt2.
\]

The two components have opposite eigenvalues of \(Z_1Z_2Z_3\). After removing a global phase,

\[
|\psi\rangle=(|000\rangle+e^{i\Phi}|111\rangle)/\sqrt2,
\quad
\boxed{\Phi=128\beta a^3bq_0g_1g_2g_3+O(\beta^2).}
\]

With the previous calibrated dephasing visibility \(v\), measure X, X, Y on the three qubits. The full probability is

\[
P(x_1,x_2,x_3)=\frac18[1+v\sin\Phi\,x_1x_2x_3].
\]

The individual and fixed-pair output states remain phase-blind for this prepared GHZ family; the parity \(W=x_1x_2x_3\) has mean \(v\sin\Phi\). Changing probe preparation, sharing a missing observer's record, or using a different physical measurement protocol is not covered by that local-blindness statement.

The earlier checkpoint is the readout layer. The additional assumptions in this document supply a conditional encoding layer, not a derivation of a microscopic theory.

### Physical control reversal

Changing \(q_0\) to \(-q_0\) reverses the retained three-body phase. Setting \(q_0=0\), or decoupling any one qubit, removes it. Scaling both pulse areas by \(u\) gives a leading \(u^4\) signal. These are physical parameter changes, not merely reversal of the recorded outcome sign.

They are useful consistency checks but are not exclusive quantum-gravity signatures.

## 6. Verification actually performed

The Python program implements four independent checks:

1. Exact symbolic decomposition of the eight spin-branch phases: only the identity and \(Z_1Z_2Z_3\) coefficients survive.
2. Direct finite-Fock-matrix multiplication of all 32 oscillator pulses, with dimensions 32, 48, 64 and 96; unequal-coupling and thermal-state checks are additional tests.
3. Gaussian momentum quadrature for complete branch coherence, including static nuisances, a control imbalance, and the next momentum-polynomial term.
4. Binomial sampling of the resulting fleet probabilities, including 20,000 repeated 100,000-trial experiments for each of three hypotheses.

A finite matrix cannot satisfy a canonical commutator globally. The matrix check is therefore a convergence check on specified low-energy states, not a proof of an exact finite-dimensional canonical algebra.

Benchmark values:

\[
a=b=0.5,\quad q_0=g_1=g_2=g_3=1,\quad
\beta=0.0025,\quad v=0.8.
\]

This deliberately large toy deformation gives \(\Phi=0.02\) radians. It is not a Planck-sized parameter for the physical scale example below.

| Model or control | Calculated phase, radians |
|---|---:|
| Canonical mechanics, ideal pulses | 0 |
| Chosen deformation | +0.020000 |
| Canonical mechanics, matching cubic actuator nonlinearity | +0.020000 |
| Common drive bias reversed | -0.020000 |
| Common drive bias off | 0 |
| One sensor uncoupled | 0, to numerical precision |
| Canonical mechanics, 0.1% area imbalance in one sign block | +0.006000 |

Across the four finite-matrix sizes, errors in the complex coherence were below \(1.4\times10^{-13}\). The unequal-coupling and thermal matrix checks passed their recorded tolerances. One hundred randomly selected static diagonal single/pair nuisance Hamiltonians also passed the cancellation check. These are numerical checks of the idealized model, not experimental error bars.

### Finite-shot recovery

For each repeated experiment, 50,000 trials were used at each sign of \(q_0\), preserving the previous budget of 100,000 GHZ preparations and 300,000 local readings. A fixed common readout offset of 0.03 radians was added. The local-branch estimator was

\[
\widehat\Phi=\tfrac12\{\arcsin(\widehat C_+/v)-\arcsin(\widehat C_-/v)\},
\qquad \widehat\beta_{\rm eff}=\widehat\Phi/8.
\]

Across 20,000 simulated experiments, the deformation case gave

\[
\operatorname{mean}(\widehat\beta_{\rm eff})=0.00249677,
\quad \operatorname{SD}(\widehat\beta_{\rm eff})=0.00049557.
\]

The conventional-actuator mimic gave mean 0.00250266 and standard deviation 0.00049760. The small differences between those Monte Carlo summaries are sampling fluctuations: their underlying distributions are exactly identical in this model.

The null model gave mean 0.000000571 and standard deviation 0.00049115. These results check parameter recovery, not the probability that a theory is true. The sine/arcsine readout is local; unbounded phases require an independently budgeted second quadrature or an unwrapping protocol.

### Perturbation-order audit

The local expansion of \(P=\tan(\sqrt\beta p)/\sqrt\beta\) adds \(2\beta^2p^5/15\) to the momentum generator. We tested this next polynomial term, **not** a global tangent-operator theory with its domain and boundary issues.

For the equal-coupling benchmark, the resulting branch phase is

\[
\Phi(p)=128\beta a^3b+512\beta^2a^3bp^2+1024\beta^2a^5b.
\]

For a centered Gaussian momentum distribution of variance \(V\), the coherence is

\[
\eta=\exp\{i[128\beta a^3b+1024\beta^2a^5b]\}
(1-2i\,512\beta^2a^3bV)^{-1/2}.
\]

At \(V=0.5\), the phase is approximately 0.020200 radians: about a 1% correction to the first-order toy result. The first-order answer is not represented as an all-orders prediction. At larger momentum variance, the higher-order dependence grows and the leading thermal cancellation is not exact.

## 7. The exact conventional counterexample

Consider ordinary canonical mechanics \([x,p]=i\), but let the momentum control couple to

\[
P_{\rm actuator}=p+\frac{\eta}{3}p^3.
\]

No modified quantum kinematics are needed for this Hamiltonian. Comparing \(\eta=\beta\) with the first-order GUP representation shows that the pulse generators themselves are identical, so all allowed pulse sequences, oscillator states, qubit outcomes and correlations in this model are identical.

Allowing both effects gives, to retained order,

\[
P_{\rm pulse}=p+\frac{\beta+\eta}{3}p^3,
\qquad
\Phi=128(\beta+\eta)a^3bq_0g_1g_2g_3.
\]

The fleet estimates \(\beta_{\rm eff}=\beta+\eta\). It cannot separately estimate \(\beta\) and \(\eta\) with these controls. The equality is exact between the specified polynomial models and perturbative when one model is interpreted as the full GUP.

Our multi-control numerical design used signs \(q_0=\pm1\) and amplitude scales \(u=0.7,1,1.3\). Its local phase response to \((\beta,\eta,\epsilon,b_{\rm readout})\) is

\[
[8s u^4,\;8s u^4,\;6s u^2,\;1].
\]

The response matrix has rank 3, not 4: the two nonlinear columns coincide. Combining \(\beta+\eta\) into a single parameter restores full rank for the three remaining effective parameters. Thus the amplitude scan can distinguish this simple area mismatch from an effective cubic response but cannot attribute that response to quantum gravity.

This is a counterexample within an explicit nuisance class. It does not prove that every conceivable microscopic model is experimentally indistinguishable, or that additional independently characterized measurements cannot distinguish the hypotheses.

Higher-order ordinary optomechanical Hamiltonians are already a documented serious issue for this class of proposed tests [2].

### One-block pulse imbalance

At \(\beta=\eta=0\), let the first signed-loop block have \(a\to a(1+\epsilon)\), leaving the other three unchanged. With the equal couplings and bias above,

\[
\Phi_{\rm leakage}=24ab\epsilon.
\]

For \(a=b=0.5\) and \(\epsilon=0.001\), this is 0.006 radians. It reverses with the common bias too, so bias reversal alone is not sufficient. Its \(u^2\) amplitude scaling differs from the leading \(u^4\) response if the fractional error remains constant across the scan.

## 8. Translating the model into a length-scale parameter

The conventional physical parametrization is [1,4]

\[
[x_{\rm phys},p_{\rm phys}]
=i\hbar\left[1+\beta_0\left(\frac{p_{\rm phys}}{M_Pc}\right)^2\right].
\]

For a mechanical oscillator define

\[
p_*=\sqrt{\hbar m\omega},\qquad x_*=\sqrt{\hbar/(m\omega)},
\qquad \beta=\beta_0\frac{\hbar m\omega}{(M_Pc)^2}.
\]

Under this **particular full positive-parameter GUP model**, the minimum-position-uncertainty scale is \(\ell_{\min}=\sqrt{\beta_0}\ell_P\). This is not a universal statement about spacetime pixels. Applying a deformation to a composite oscillator's center-of-mass mode is a further physical assumption; it is not automatically justified by an elementary-particle model [1].

Using NIST values \(M_P=2.176434\times10^{-8}\) kg and \(\ell_P=1.616255\times10^{-35}\) m [5,6], and **assuming** \(m=10^{-11}\) kg and \(\omega/2\pi=10^5\) Hz solely for scale comparison,

\[
\beta/\beta_0=1.5564085934\times10^{-41}.
\]

With the modest benchmark pulse strengths:

| Assumed \(\beta_0\) | Model minimum length | Leading phase |
|---:|---|---:|
| 1 | \(\ell_P\) | \(1.24513\times10^{-40}\) rad |
| 0.01 | \(0.1\ell_P\) | \(1.24513\times10^{-42}\) rad |

The toy parameter 0.0025 corresponds formally to \(\beta_0\approx1.61\times10^{38}\) with these scales. It was chosen to test the protocol, not to simulate a detectable Planck-strength effect.

For fixed visibility 0.8, fixed modest pulse strengths, independent trials, no systematics, and a small phase, the trial count for an *expected shift of five null standard errors* is \(N\simeq25/(v^2\Phi^2)\). It is approximately \(2.52\times10^{81}\) for \(\beta_0=1\), and \(2.52\times10^{85}\) for \(\beta_0=0.01\). These are conditional algebraic examples, not a universal lower bound or a specified detection-power criterion.

### Why larger pulses are not free amplification

The phase increases as \(a^3b\). Algebraically, setting \(a=b\approx1.78002\times10^9\), with the other normalized couplings still unity, would give 0.02 radians for \(\beta_0=1\) in the ideal model. The largest conditional kick would be approximately \(7.12\times10^9\) dimensionless units, corresponding to a position-translation scale of about 29.2 micrometers for the assumed oscillator.

This is not a demonstrated implementation. With this amplification, the simple one-block imbalance produces signal-sized leakage at a fractional area error of about \(2.63\times10^{-22}\). Real pulse generation, nonlinearities, coherence, free evolution and reference calibration must all be modeled before proposing a useful sensitivity. No such precision has been demonstrated by this calculation.

## 9. What would resolve the remaining question?

To attribute a measured effective response to the proposed commutator, the nuisance Hamiltonian must be independently constrained or the theory must predict another measured dependence unavailable to the nuisance model. Simply adding amplitudes, reversing the bias, repeating the same channel, adding observers, or relabeling the residual does not solve the exact \(\beta+\eta\) degeneracy.

A complete research proposal would need an actual oscillator/actuator model, calibrated nonlinear terms with uncertainties, free-evolution dynamics, finite-duration controls, correlated errors, and a resource comparison with established alternatives. It would also need a derivation connecting an underlying geometric theory to the proposed commutator and its parameter normalization. This checkpoint supplies none of those by assumption.

The useful deliverable is a verified selective-response calculation and its explicit failure mode. It is not a claim that no future physical interaction can succeed.

## 10. Reproduction

Run normal Python (not optimized `python -O`, which disables the test assertions):

```sh
python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
python commutator_controls.py --output .
```

On systems with many BLAS threads, setting `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1` and `OMP_NUM_THREADS=1` avoids unnecessary thread overhead. Random seed: 20260905. The results file records package versions, configuration, convergence values, raw-triple summaries, rank checks and resources. Monte Carlo draws may differ across numerical-library versions.

Files include the full source, machine-readable results, scenario CSV, eight-outcome raw-triple count CSV, frozen configuration, and SHA-256 checksums. No claim of a research-independent replication is made; the numerical checks were run here.
